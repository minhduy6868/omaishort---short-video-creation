I found his phone on the counter at 2:17 a.m. He said he was sleeping. The lock screen was a photo of us. The messages were not.

Her name was Mara. She asked if I would be home this weekend. He typed, Don't worry. She never checks.

I stood in our kitchen and watched my hands stop shaking. I took one screenshot. Then I put the phone back exactly as I found it, next to the keys, next to the lie.

In the morning he kissed my forehead and asked if I slept well. I said yes. I packed his favorite shirt for the trip he thought I didn't know about.

At noon I sent Mara the screenshot. At twelve-oh-one I sent it to his mother. At twelve-oh-two I changed the locks.

He called from the airport. I let it ring. The last thing he hears when the voicemail picks up is me, calm, telling him the house is no longer his, and neither am I.
